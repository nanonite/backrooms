# Walkable "Braindance" From a Video: Feasibility, Prior Art, and the Real Fix for the Collision Handoff

## TL;DR
- **Your central premise is wrong: Vista4D does not solve your physics problem.** Vista4D is a *novel-view video synthesis* (appearance) tool — it outputs videos, never collision geometry. The splat→mesh→collider handoff that is actually breaking happens *after* Vista4D and is untouched by it. You would be paying a heavy compute cost (full Wan2.1-T2V-14B base, ~30GB on disk, plus a stated 24GB-VRAM preprocessing step) to improve the wrong half of the pipeline.
- **The collision handoff is a solved, well-documented problem, and a reference implementation already exists:** PlayCanvas's `splat-transform -K` voxelizes a splat and flood-fills a watertight `.collision.glb`; the floor is found by RANSAC plane fitting; scale/up-axis are fixed by an explicit similarity transform; and the monolithic-trimesh "panic" is fixed by CoACD convex decomposition + decimation (or a heightfield floor). None of this needs a diffusion model.
- **Recommendation: abandon Vista4D for v1, and pick the engine with the most mature splat+physics integration.** For pure reproducibility on your RTX 4070 Ti, **PlayCanvas/SuperSplat** is the single most de-risked path (the exact use case has a public, forkable FPS demo). If you want a desktop engine, **Unity + UnityGaussianSplatting (Aras Pranckevičius)** is the best-documented native option. Given output quality is explicitly not a priority, also seriously consider a **photogrammetry textured-mesh** path, which gives you working collision *for free*.

## Key Findings

1. **Vista4D is real, but it is a video tool, not a geometry tool.** It is a CVPR 2026 Highlight from Eyeline Labs/Netflix (arXiv 2604.21915, released 2026-04-23; proceedings pp. 32671–32682; Highlight poster #377, Sun Jun 7 2026). Its pipeline is: source video → Pi3X or Depth-Anything-3 (4D reconstruction) → SAM3 (dynamic/static segmentation) → point-cloud render in target cameras → a Wan2.1-T2V-14B finetune that **outputs novel-view videos** (`.mp4`). Its documented outputs are videos, depth maps, and alpha/dynamic masks — *not* meshes and *not* colliders. Its stated applications (per the official project page) are "dynamic scene expansion (casual video capture of scene as background reference), 4D scene recomposition (point cloud editing), and long video inference with memory" — all appearance/editing, none physics.

2. **The fall-through and far-spawn bugs are scale/alignment + collision-topology bugs, fully independent of the front-end.** They are caused by (a) COLMAP/splat space being arbitrary-scale and arbitrary-orientation vs the engine's metric Y-up/Z-up world, and (b) feeding a huge concave triangle mesh straight to a physics engine. Both have standard, documented fixes.

3. **The reference "splat → walkable game" implementation already exists and is public.** PlayCanvas engineer Iakov Sumygin's "Turning a Gaussian Splat Into a Videogame" (2026-04-22) ships a browser FPS built on a downloaded splat, with collision from `splat-transform -K`, a Recast navmesh, baked lighting probes and NPCs — the entire PlayCanvas project is public and forkable.

4. **Wan2.1-14B can technically run on 12GB, but the Vista4D *preprocessing* step is reported to need 24GB VRAM** — which is the real blocker on your card, separate from inference.

## Details

### A. What Vista4D actually is and does (factual verification)
Vista4D is genuine, high-quality research (CVPR 2026 Highlight; arXiv 2604.21915; repo github.com/Eyeline-Labs/Vista4D, Apache-2.0). The abstract describes it as "a robust and flexible video reshooting framework that grounds the input video and target cameras in a 4D point cloud … given an input video, our method re-synthesizes the scene with the same dynamics from a different camera trajectory and viewpoint." Every described output is a **video** plus auxiliary depth/mask data. There is no mesh export, no collider export, and no claim anywhere of producing collision geometry.

The front of its pipeline (Pi3X) does produce camera poses and a point cloud. Pi3X "supports metric scale reconstruction (approximate)" — note the *approximate* qualifier; the original π³ predicts "affine-invariant camera poses and scale-invariant local point maps," i.e., arbitrary scale by default. This is exactly the source of a fall-forever / spawn-far-away bug if scale is not explicitly recovered.

The multi-trajectory → multi-splat → stitch idea is **the user's own untested extension.** Vista4D supports rendering multiple hand-designed target cameras (via its Viser-based camera UI) and even point-cloud editing, but nothing in the repo, paper, or any third-party demo shows training separate splats per trajectory and stitching them for collision coverage. It would be novel work, not a supported feature.

**VRAM reality on a 12GB card (from the repo + WanGP):** The official Eyeline code targets multi-GPU setups (its inference supports "USP multi-GPU inference (with USE_USP=true and setting NUM_GPUS)," default `NUM_GPUS=8`) and 24GB+/datacenter cards (its arch guide tops out at RTX 4090/L40-class), and it warns that a Flash-Attention misconfiguration "[causes] out of memory (OOM) errors when doing Vista4D inference." The WanGP/Wan2GP wrapper (v11.52, 2026-05-02) is the only path that brings Vista4D into consumer range: WanGP states the unoptimized model "requires x3 the amount of VRAM than what is usually required for equivalent output res and the preprocessing needs 24 GB of VRAM to build a 4D map," but that "thanks to WanGP magic VRAM requirements have been reduced to 10 GB of VRAM or less" for inference, recommending the Lightx2v 4-step LoRA. **Crucially, the 10GB figure applies only to the diffusion inference stage; the 4D-map preprocessing is stated to need 24GB.** Vista4D also requires the full Wan2.1-T2V-14B base weights (the repo confirms its checkpoints are "finetuned on Wan-AI/Wan2.1-T2V-14B" and instructs you to download the base "into ./checkpoints/wan/Wan2.1-T2V-14B/"; ~28.6GB fp16 transformer, ~30–32GB total) in addition to its own finetune checkpoints. Realistic single-card generation time, by analogy to Wan2.1-14B (no Vista4D-specific consumer benchmark exists), is ~4 minutes per 5-second 480p clip on an RTX 4090 without quantization — and Vista4D is described as *slower* than baseline Wan2.1 unless the 4-step LoRA is applied (which could bring it to ~1–2 min/clip).

**Verdict on the central claim:** "Vista4D adds way more ability for the pipeline to render the physics" is mistaken. Vista4D improves *appearance/coverage*. Denser, more complete visual coverage can *indirectly* help mesh extraction (fewer holes in floors → fewer fall-through gaps), but it does not perform, fix, or even touch the splat→mesh→collider→alignment handoff that is actually failing. The compute cost is disproportionate to that indirect benefit, and the preprocessing VRAM wall likely exceeds your 12GB anyway.

### B. The real fix for the collision handoff (engine-agnostic, the most important section)

**(1) Coordinate frame, up-axis, and scale — fix the spawn-far-away and fall-forever bugs.**
- Your splat lives in COLMAP/Pi3 space, which is arbitrary scale and not gravity-aligned. The "player spawned very far away" bug is almost always because the reconstruction's origin is not the scene's center and the units are not meters. Fix it with an explicit similarity transform (scale s, rotation R, translation t) applied once, offline, to both the splat and the extracted mesh so they share one engine-world frame.
- **Find the floor programmatically, don't eyeball it.** Run RANSAC plane fitting on the point cloud / mesh vertices: downsample to ~5 cm, keep the lowest ~50% of points as floor candidates, run RANSAC enforcing a near-vertical normal (|n_z| > 0.9), pick the plane with the most inliers. That plane defines the up-axis (rotate so the floor normal becomes engine-up) and the floor height (translate so the floor sits at y=0). Spawn the player a fixed offset (e.g., 1.0 m) above that plane at the centroid of the floor inliers. (This RANSAC-floor recipe is standard practice in current monocular-reconstruction papers and in tools like the Colmapview viewer.)
- **Recover metric scale.** Pi3X gives approximate metric scale; if you trust it, you're done. Otherwise set scale by a known real-world distance between two points, or by COLMAP's `model_aligner` with reference coordinates (it estimates a 3D similarity transform via RANSAC). Getting units to meters is what makes a standard character controller's gravity, step height, and capsule radius behave sanely.
- **Y-up vs Z-up:** COLMAP/OpenCV use a Y-down camera convention; Blender/COLMAP world is often Z-up; Unity is Y-up left-handed; Unreal is Z-up left-handed; Godot/three.js/PlayCanvas are Y-up right-handed. Bake the axis conversion into the same one-time transform. The drag-and-drop "Colmapview" viewer exposes 9 coordinate-system presets (COLMAP, OpenCV, Three.js, OpenGL, Vulkan, Blender, Houdini, Unity, Unreal) plus 1-point origin, 2-point scale, and 3-point RANSAC floor alignment — a convenient way to compute this transform interactively before exporting.

**(2) Why the monolithic high-poly trimesh "panics," and the correct alternatives.**
A single giant concave triangle-soup collider is the worst case for any physics engine: narrow-phase queries against thousands of triangles are slow, and characters tunnel through triangle *edges/seams* (a long-documented failure — Unity's own issue tracker has "Game object partially falls through convex mesh collider … reproducible near convex mesh collider's edges," and Unity's guidance is that convex mesh colliders must not be used for static floors; use a non-convex mesh collider or box/compound colliders). The correct alternatives, in order of robustness for your "static walkable" case:
- **Voxelize + flood-fill to a watertight collision mesh** (what PlayCanvas `splat-transform -K` does): per the PlayCanvas blog, the `-K`/`--collision-mesh` flag "voxelizes the splat, flood-fills the navigable interior from a seed position, and writes out a watertight .collision.glb that you can import straight into PlayCanvas as a mesh collider" (`splat-transform scene.ply -K scene.sog`). This is purpose-built for exactly your case and is the single highest-leverage tool you are not yet using.
- **Convex decomposition with CoACD** (the modern successor to the now-EOL V-HACD): breaks the concave mesh into a set of convex hulls, which every engine collides against quickly and robustly. Per the CoACD paper (Wei, Liu et al., "Approximate Convex Decomposition for 3D Meshes with Collision-Aware Concavity and Tree Search," ACM TOG 41(4), SIGGRAPH 2022), the method "is better to preserve collision conditions of the input shape with fewer components … [supporting] delicate and efficient object interaction in downstream applications." (Documented trade-off: CoACD can slightly inflate geometry and runs 1–2 orders of magnitude slower to compute than V-HACD, which avoids the inflation — either is fine offline.)
- **Decimate first.** Reduce the extracted mesh to a reasonable poly count before decomposition — your output quality bar is low, so aggressive decimation is free.
- **Heightfield collider for the floor.** For a largely flat walkable ground, a heightfield/terrain collider is the most stable and cheapest floor of all and eliminates tunneling.
- **Chunk it.** Split the collider into spatial tiles so no single collider is enormous; this also helps streaming.

**(3) Walkable-surface / navmesh generation.**
Once you have a clean collision mesh, generate a navmesh with Recast (the PlayCanvas demo feeds its `scene.collision.glb` straight into recast-navigation: "the collision mesh already represents 'solid floor you can walk on', so Recast just has to rasterize it, filter walkable spans and build the nav polygons"). Godot and Unity both bake navmeshes from collision geometry natively. The navmesh also gives you a guaranteed-valid spawn point (any walkable polygon centroid).

### C. Engine recommendation (Bevy is rejected; maximize reproducibility)

**Winner for pure reproducibility of *this* task: PlayCanvas + SuperSplat.** It is the only stack where your exact end-to-end goal — splat for visuals + collision mesh + FPS controller — exists as a *public, forkable, working project*. `splat-transform -K` solves the collision handoff in one CLI command; SuperSplat itself now has built-in collision generation and a "walk mode"; everything is open source; and "works on my machine" reproducibility is excellent because it runs in a browser tab and the project ships as static files. The cost: it's web/JS, not a native desktop engine, and large splats need streamed LOD.

**Best desktop option: Unity + UnityGaussianSplatting (Aras Pranckevičius).** This is the most mature, best-documented native splat renderer (MIT; release v1.1.1, 3.3k stars / ~449 forks; self-described "Toy Gaussian Splatting visualization in Unity"; reads original-3DGS PLY and Scaniverse SPZ — v1.1.1 fixed SPZ rotation parsing and added more flexible PLY parsing). It sits alongside Unity's battle-tested CharacterController, mesh/convex colliders, and navmesh baking — all heavily represented in documentation and training corpora, which aids reproducibility. Caveats: it requires a modern GPU API (DX12/Vulkan/Metal; DX11/OpenGL unsupported — fine on your 4070 Ti with Vulkan on Ubuntu), it is explicitly a visualization integration (you wire up collision yourself from a separately-extracted mesh), and Unity version-matching can still bite (pin the LTS the package targets, currently the 2022.3 / Unity 6 LTS line).

**Unreal: capable but heavier.** UE5 has Luma/XScene/other splat plugins, and Polycam documents a full splat+collision import flow (import GLTF collision mesh, set "Use Complex Collision as Simple," mark "Actor Hidden in Game," align to splat). Their own troubleshooting lists "Character Falls Through: double-check that complex collision is enabled and the GLTF mesh is properly aligned with the splat" — i.e., the same handoff problem, with manual alignment. Powerful but more moving parts than you need.

**Godot: improving fast but still youngest.** Native Godot 3DGS plugins now exist (GDGS / ReconWorldLab's compositor-based plugin for Godot 4.4+, MIT; plus 2Retr0's and haztro's compute-based viewers). GDGS even auto-centers imported data and "applies a one-time default Z correction" on new nodes, which directly mitigates your far-spawn problem. Godot's CharacterBody3D + built-in navmesh are clean. But the splat plugins are newer and less documented than Unity's, so reproducibility is slightly lower today. Godot is the strongest *open-source desktop* choice if you prefer it ideologically.

### D. Simpler, more reproducible alternative paths (strongly recommended to evaluate first)

**Path 1 — The plain robust splat path (no diffusion model).** Capture or VGGT/Pi3 poses → train splat → extract mesh with **2DGS** (flat disks that align to surfaces, cleanest geometry, "much less noise") or **SuGaR** (surface-alignment regularization, then Poisson reconstruction) → decimate + CoACD → mature engine, with the alignment done right per section B. This keeps splat visuals and is dramatically more reproducible than adding Vista4D.

**Path 2 — Photogrammetry textured mesh (the most reproducible of all).** Run the same video through RealityCapture/RealityScan or Meshroom to get a **textured triangle mesh directly**. Because you get an actual surface, **collision is free** — the mesh *is* the collider (after decimation/decomposition). This is the honest trade: as multiple sources put it, "photogrammetry produces a triangle mesh with texture maps … you can … 3D print the result," whereas "a splat is just a cloud of oriented blobs — no triangles, no colliders, no navmesh." Splats look better on vegetation/reflective surfaces and process faster; meshes look worse but have guaranteed-working physics. **Given that you explicitly do not care about output quality and you do care about a working pipeline, this is very likely your most reproducible answer.** You can skip Gaussian splatting entirely for a static walkable scene.

**Path 3 — Hybrid (best of both, if you want the splat look).** Use the photogrammetry/2DGS mesh purely as the invisible collider (set "hidden in game"), and render the Gaussian splat on top for visuals. This is exactly the PlayCanvas pattern (visible splat + invisible `.collision.glb`).

### E. Where Vista4D *could* legitimately help (the steelman)
If, after you have a robust pipeline, you find that your single source video leaves genuine holes in the floor or walls (under-observed regions that produce mesh gaps you keep falling through), Vista4D's novel-view synthesis is a *defensible* way to manufacture extra training views to densify those regions before splat training — the same logic as its "dynamic scene expansion." But that is an optimization for *coverage completeness*, applied only if and when hole-filling is your bottleneck, and it does not replace any step in section B. Treat it as a possible v3 enhancement, not a v1 dependency — and budget for the 24GB preprocessing wall.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Prove the handoff on a known-good asset (≈1 day).** Download a Creative-Commons splat from SuperSplat, run `splat-transform scene.ply -K scene.sog`, drop the `.collision.glb` into PlayCanvas (or your chosen engine) as a static mesh rigidbody, and walk around. This isolates the collision/alignment logic from all reconstruction noise and gives you a working baseline immediately. *Benchmark to proceed: player spawns on the floor and cannot fall through.*

**Stage 1 — Pick your engine.** If browser/JS is acceptable, **stay in PlayCanvas** — you're effectively done after Stage 0. If you need desktop, port to **Unity + UnityGaussianSplatting** (or Godot + GDGS if you want open-source). Reuse the Stage-0 collision GLB.

**Stage 2 — Replace the asset with your own capture, alignment done right.** Run your video → Pi3/VGGT poses → splat. Then, *before* engine import, run the offline alignment script from section B: RANSAC floor → rotate to up-axis → translate floor to y=0 → set metric scale → export aligned splat + aligned collision mesh together. *Benchmark to proceed: no far-spawn, no fall-through, on your own scene.*

**Stage 3 — Fix any remaining tunneling with CoACD/heightfield/chunking.** Only if the voxel collision mesh still tunnels: decimate, run CoACD convex decomposition, or drop a heightfield floor. Bake a Recast/engine navmesh and use a walkable-polygon centroid as the spawn point.

**Stage 4 — (Optional) Looping dynamic splats.** For the "braindance" appearance-only pedestrians/cars, layer a small dynamic/4D splat or a looping billboard/video texture on top of the static scene, never added to the collider. This is the only part where a 4D method is even conceptually relevant — and even here you don't need Vista4D; a simple looping element suffices.

**Decline Vista4D for now.** Re-open the question only if (a) Stages 0–3 work and (b) you have measured, specific floor/wall holes from insufficient coverage that better view synthesis would fill — and even then, verify WanGP can run the *preprocessing* on 12GB before committing.

**Thresholds that would change this advice:** If you acquire a 24GB+ GPU, the Vista4D preprocessing wall disappears and the coverage-densification steelman (section E) becomes practical. If your scenes are large *outdoor* captures with severe occlusion holes, view synthesis matters more and photogrammetry matters less. If you decide output quality *does* matter after all, lean splat (PlayCanvas/Unity) over photogrammetry mesh.

## Caveats
- **No public prior art for Vista4D-as-game-front-end.** No GitHub project, blog, video, or forum thread was found showing anyone building a walkable game from Vista4D specifically. The repo is very new (released 2026-04-23, ~40 stars, 2 commits at time of research) and the multi-trajectory-stitch workflow is the user's own untested idea. Prior art exists abundantly for the *general* splat-in-engine case (PlayCanvas FPS, Unity/Unreal/Godot plugins), not the Vista4D-specific case.
- **The "10GB VRAM" Vista4D figure is WanGP's claim for the inference stage only**, and no independent 12GB user benchmark was found; the stated 24GB preprocessing requirement is a likely blocker on your card and should be tested before any commitment.
- **Pi3X metric scale is "approximate"** by the authors' own description; do not assume the reconstruction is correctly scaled — always verify and, if needed, set scale explicitly.
- **CoACD vs V-HACD trade-offs** (inflation, compute time) are documented above; pick per scene. Convex decomposition smooths over fine concavities, which is fine for a walkable floor but can make thin railings non-solid — use the voxel/flood-fill or heightfield approach where thin solids matter.
- Engine plugin maturity and version compatibility shift quickly; pin exact versions of UnityGaussianSplatting / GDGS / Unity LTS in your repo for reproducibility.
